Brushstroke Setup

Setting Up Brushstroke Tools

The Final step for the completed look is to use "Brushstroke Tools" to add floating brushstrokes to the model. This important as breaks up the clean edges of the 3D model. Think of it as a blur effect the farther away the model is from the camera the more "brushy" the model will look

  • Press [N] on the keyboard to bring up the N Side Panel
  • Select "Brushstroke Tools"
  • Click on "Fill"

Warning

This add-on is very intensive so I would not recommend this method very complex environments or if your computer is not very strong.


Consider compositing options that can mimic the same idea (i.e Kuwahara) I decided not to focus on compositing as to make this part of the guide as "nodeless" as possible

Fill Brushstrokes

  • After clicking "Fill" use the pen tool draw the flow
  • Draw flow in the desired shape of the object

Note

  • If the strokes are not being drawn on the model or acting wonky in any way make sure that Surface Offset at the top of blender is set to 0
  • If the flow needs to be edited again click on the "Edit Flow" button in the "Brushstroke Tools" panel to continue drawing

Adding Texture as Brushstroke

  • In the "Brushstroke Tools" panel scroll down to "Color" and click the ">" button
  • in the "Method:" row press on the drop down and select "Texture"
  • Select the texture of the painted object that was baked in the Ucupaint section

Tweaking brushstrokes

  • In the "Brushstroke Tools" panel scroll down to the "Brushstrokes" drop drown and click the ">"
  • Go to Surface and click ">"
  • Adjust the "Normal Offset" to desired to give rougher edges to the model
  • Further tweak other setting until the desired result is achieved

Note

  • I would highly recommend watching the video made by the BrushStroke Tools Team for more information on the add-on as there is not enough time to talk of all the features in it:https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fKd-ViC1ZjA
  • I'd recommend with Brush Width, Length and Scale sliders in the "Brush" tab the "Density" Slider in the "Distribution" tab and the "Stroke Variation" tab
