Texture Paint

Linking to An External Image Editing Software

After having completed a model. Blender will first be linked to an external software of choice for painting and other features.

Note

Your model must be UV unwrapped for this process. Remember this is Not a 3D modeling guide

  • Go to [Edit]>[Preferences]
  • Click on [File Paths]
  • Scroll down to [Applications] and expand the selection
  • Navigate to Image Editor and click on the Folder icon
  • Find your image editing software of choice and select it

Different Texture Painting Methods

There are a few different ways to go about painting the mode there will be three methods.

Note

The first method is my recommended way going about it but the others way are helpful as well

Method 1

Camera Setup

First is to make a camera in your 3D scene:

  • Press [Shift+A] to pull up the [Add] panel navigate and click on [Camera]
  • Set up camera to the desired location

Note

  • Press [N] on your keyboard to bring up the side panel and click on the [View] tab scroll down to [view lock] and click on [Camera to View]
  • I would aim for very static 3/4 quarter downshots and use a longer lens (50mm-75m) to prevent distortion when applying textures back onto models

Warning

Once you set up your camera do NOT move it! I would recommend unchecking [view lock] after you have decided on camera placement

  • Once the camera is placed, navigate to [View] at the top of the UI and press on [Viewport Render Image]
  • Save the image to a memorable location

Info

Paint on top of the screenshot in an external image editor application. Once you've made the desired changes, turn off the viewport render image layer and then save over the original image

Setting Up Second UVs

To get the painted image onto the model:

  • Switch to the camera view
  • With﻿ Each object that has been painted in the image editor; Select the [Object Data] tab on the right side.
  • Scroll down to [UV Maps] expand it and click on the [+] to create a new UV Map
  • Select this UV Map and make sure the camera symbol is on
  • Click on an object press [tab] to switch to Edit Mode
  • Press [A] to select the entire mesh
  • Press [U] to bring up the UV mapping window up
  • Click on [Project from View]

Info

This method projects the UVs directly from the camera view that was previously set by the camera. So your image will be perfectly mapped on the model

Importing the Image with Ucupaint

  • Open the side panel by pressing [N] scroll and click on [Ucupaint]
  • Click on [Quick Ucupaint Node Setup]
  • Go to "Type:" and switch from [Principled] to [Emission]
  • In the Layers drop down click on the [+]
  • Navigate to and click on [Open Image...]
  • Locate the desired painted image in the file explorer

Note

If your UVs are not accurate go to the "Vector" drop down and click on the second UV drop down. Here you will see the UVs you have set previously in the object Data tab

Troubleshooting

The image projects through the surface of the object this makes it hard to use this method alone and paint from multiple different angles, as you will constantly have to erase the areas a new painting projects through. This is partially solved in the next session but has some of it's own issues

Applying Image to Original UV

  • Click the [+] and then click [New image] to add a new texture layer to the object

Note

I usually use a resolution of 2048 or above and I check UDIM Tiles but I am not sure if UDIM helps with the texture painting process as I still don't know how they fully function yet. Just that they are helpful for keeping texture detail on mesh of varying geometry density

  • In the "Vector" drop down select your original proper UV unwrapped model.
  • Switch to the "Texture Paint" tab at the top
  • Go to the top right of the Texture Paint UI and click on [Options]
  • In the [Options] drop down change the "Screen Grab size" to the same aspect ratio of your camera
  • Make sure the desired layer is chosen (i.e the new layer made with the original UVs)
  • Click on [Quick Edit]
  • This will open your image editor of choice
  • Go back into blender and click [Options] in Texture Paint mode once again
  • Click [Apply]
  • Repeat this process until all areas are covered as needed

Note

  • When making a quick edit image be aware that blender will save a .png each time it is pressed in wherever the original .blend file is
  • If blender does not remember you exported image you can click (Image data can not be found) you can click on [Apply Camera Image] which will bring up a list of previously exported scree grabs to select from
  • You can also paint new details onto your model with your image editor of choice and apply them onto the model. Make sure to make a new layer on top for the new painted details and hide the screen grab layer before importing back into blender
  • Be aware this method struggles with getting the texture onto steep angles of a model (see Method 2)

Troubleshooting

While this solve the issue of Project from View UV showing through the model there are two issues.

  1. Applying the image back from an external image editor degrades the quality of the texture
    1. This can be solved partially by screen grabbing with a higher resolution but SAME aspect ratio than the actual camera render (i.e the camera resolution render at 1920x1080 but the screen grab is 3840x2160
    2. Getting very close when quick editing and applying also helps but these options are not perfect
  3. Color changes from the original opened image compared to the applied image through screenshot, I believe this is a form of color space issue however I am not sure how to solve this issue as of now or if there is a completely different way to avoid this issue

Direct Painting onto Model

  • Select the desired object
  • Switch to the "Texture Paint" tab at the top
  • press [N] to bring up the side panel and select "Ucupaint"
  • navigate to the Layers panel and press the [+] to make a new layer
  • Select any brush at the bottom of the screen and click on the object to begin painting

Note

Make sure you are painting on the original properly UV unwrapped UVs so the textures do not project through the model

Method 2

Setting up Quick Edit for painting

  • Click on the [Texture Paint] at the top of blender
  • In the top of the right of the [Texture Paint] window click on [Options]
  • In the [Options] set the screen grab size to the same aspect ratio of the camera
  • Go to in camera view
  • Click on [Quick Edit]
  • This will give a screen grab that can be painted on top of in an image editor of choice

Note

You can get a screen grab outside of the camera view but if you move your viewport you will lose the proper up angle to apply the textures back on later. It is much safer to get screen grabs from cameras you have set up first


When painting in your image software make a new layer above your screen grab and hide the screen grab layer once you are done painting so only the edits you made are applied to the model

Using UCUpaint to Import

  • Once the desired screen grab is painted save the image and return to blender
  • Press [N] to bring up the side panel and select "Ucupaint"
  • Navigate to the Layers panel and press the [+] and select [New Image] to create a new layer to apply the edits
  • Click on [Options] once again and then click [Apply Camera Image]

Troubleshooting 1

When applying the image the colors become significantly desaturated. This issue requires more trouble shooting. I would recommend method one as color accuracy is preserved better

Troubleshooting 2

When applying at steep angles or far away distances, this method tends to struggle applying the texture

Method 3

Direct Painting onto Model

  • Select the desired object
  • Switch to the "Texture Paint" tab at the top
  • press [N] to bring up the side panel and select "Ucupaint"
  • navigate to the Layers panel and press the [+] and select [New Image] to make a new layer
  • Select any brush at the bottom of the screen and click on the object to begin painting
